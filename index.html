Suomenlinna Guided Tour

Use the guided tour as the historical framework. Suomenlinna began in 1748 under Swedish rule as Sveaborg, became part of the Russian Empire after the 1808–09 conquest, and later became Suomenlinna after Finnish independence. The same physical fortress therefore served three states.

What to notice

The strategic scale of the Dry Dock; the Great Courtyard as an administrative center; the use of terrain as military architecture; and how the site’s political meaning changes under Swedish, Russian and Finnish rule.

Futuro + Civic Helsinki + the Future of the City Centre

Finnish visions of the future — from 1960s space-age architecture to contemporary public institutions and urban revitalization.

10:00 AM
REGISTEREDFree · HDW priority
Marimekko and Futuro at Helsinki Design Week

Location: Kaisaniemi Botanic Garden, Kaisaniemenranta 2.

This is a priority temporary installation, not an optional add-on. Matti Suuronen’s 1968 Futuro House — a space-age fiberglass vision of portable future living — is installed in the Botanic Garden and reinterpreted by Marimekko.

The interior uses patterns by Danish textile artist Vibeke Rohland, developed during her year-long Marimekko Artist in Residence period. The installation asks what this optimistic 1960s vision of future living means when reconsidered against today’s needs and aspirations.

Why it belongs

It brings together architecture, preservation, Finnish design history, contemporary reinterpretation and a temporary HDW-specific experience. It is also the first brand-new Futuro in roughly sixty years.

Good to know

Allow about 45–60 minutes. Registration confirmed. Capacity inside is limited, so arrive on time and allow for a possible short wait.

Understand the explosive growth of Riga around 1900 and how architecture became a vehicle for modernity and emerging Latvian identity.

Art Nouveau / National Romantic Architecture Tour

Private specialist tour focused on Riga’s Art Nouveau architecture and urban history, with particular attention to National Romanticism, the social/political context of the building boom, and the difference between highly decorative Jugendstil and architecture tied more explicitly to Latvian identity.

Façades are only the surface. Ask how new apartment typologies, materials, technologies, bourgeois life, industrial wealth and national consciousness changed the city at the same time.

Užupis

Cross into Užupis and give the neighborhood real time rather than treating it as a photo stop. Its self-declared “Republic,” artist identity and playful constitution are amusing, but the more interesting story is how a neglected district was reinvented after independence through culture, creative communities and local myth-making.

What to notice

The relationship between historic fabric and contemporary interventions; art in semi-public spaces; river edges and courtyards; and the deliberate use of humor and cultural identity as tools of post-Soviet place-making.
